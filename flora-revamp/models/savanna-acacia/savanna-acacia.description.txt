A solitary savanna tree with the unmistakable wide, flat-topped umbrella silhouette characteristic of the acacia family, far broader than it is tall in its crown spread. A single, slightly leaning trunk rises from the ground with a moderate basal flare, holding a fairly straight, columnar shaft for the lower two-thirds of the tree's height before it abruptly divides. The bark is the dominant lower surface material: a furrowed, fibrous grey-brown skin with vertical fissures and a slightly scaly, weathered texture, paler and dustier where light catches the ridges and darker in the grooves, deepening to a muted earthy brown near the base. From the upper trunk the form explodes into a dense candelabra of branches that fork repeatedly and sweep upward and outward at shallow angles, each branch tapering quickly and re-dividing into a fine, twiggy network. These slender limbs share the same grey-brown bark but thin and smooth toward their tips. The crown itself reads as a broad, plate-like canopy of foliage held almost horizontally: clusters of tiny, feathery bipinnate leaflets massed into soft, cloud-like clumps that are mid-to-bright spring green on top, lighter and more yellow-green where sun strikes, and shadowed darker green underneath. The foliage texture is delicate and lacy rather than solid, with visible gaps where branch structure shows through the leaf layers. The overall feel is airy, sun-bleached, and open. This kind of tree belongs to hot, dry tropical savanna and semi-arid grassland biomes, standing alone or sparsely scattered across open plains.
