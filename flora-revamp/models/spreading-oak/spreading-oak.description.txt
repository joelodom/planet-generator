A broad, stately deciduous shade tree presenting the classic spreading, dome-like silhouette of a mature open-grown oak, wider than it is tall, with the crown billowing outward in great rounded masses that nearly touch the ground at the lower flanks. The form is anchored by a single short, exceptionally stout trunk that flares at the base into a buttressed root collar and divides relatively low into several massive, muscular primary limbs; these heavy scaffold branches twist and angle upward and outward, subdividing into progressively finer ramification that is largely hidden beneath the foliage canopy. The trunk and major limbs carry furrowed, ridged bark in muted grey and greyish-brown tones, its blocky vertical fissures reading as a coarse, scaly texture that catches light along the ridges and falls into shadow in the grooves; the wood structure feels solid, weighty, and time-worn. Cloaking the upper two-thirds of the tree is dense, mid-to-deep green foliage gathered into distinct rounded, cumulus-like clumps separated by darker shadowed pockets and small sky-holes, giving the canopy a lumpy, textured, almost broccoli-like read rather than a smooth shell. Leaf color is a healthy summer green, slightly cooler and darker in the recesses, with subtle olive highlights where light grazes the outer leaves. The overall posture is symmetrical, grounded, and monumental. This kind of tree belongs in temperate deciduous woodland, open pasture, parkland, savanna edge, or as a freestanding specimen on a lawn, where it has room to spread its wide crown.
