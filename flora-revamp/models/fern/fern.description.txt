A clump-forming herbaceous fern presenting the classic shuttlecock or vase-shaped rosette, in which many long, slender fronds erupt from a single compact crown at the base and arch outward and upward in every direction to form a roughly hemispherical, feathery dome. The silhouette is light, lacy and airy, widest near the top where the frond tips fan out and tapering down to a narrow tufted root knot at ground contact. Each frond is a once- to twice-pinnate blade: a thin central rachis carries dozens of paired pinnae that diminish in length toward the pointed tip, giving every leaf a long lance-like, gently tapering outline; the lower pinnae are themselves cut into fine lobed segments, producing the characteristic plumose, comb-like texture. The dominant surface material is soft, matte foliage in fresh yellow-green to mid-green, slightly paler and more chartreuse at the young growing tips and deepening toward the interior; veins read as faint lighter lines and the blades have a delicate, almost translucent quality with no gloss. At the very base the materials shift: thin straw-brown to tan stipes converge into a dense, fibrous brown rootball flecked with reddish and dark earthy tones and a few stray dangling rootlets, the only woody-looking element in an otherwise wholly leafy plant. There is no flower, fruit or rigid stem — structure comes entirely from the radial spray of resilient fronds. This kind of fern belongs to damp, shaded woodland floors, streambanks, and the moist understory of temperate forests, and is a common ornamental in shade gardens and as a leafy interior or border accent.
