A low, densely branched evergreen shrub with a rounded, mounding to slightly irregular dome silhouette that is broader than it is tall, billowing outward from a compact woody base. The structure begins with a short, gnarled, multi-stemmed trunk cluster near the ground -- twisting, fibrous woody stems in a warm tan-to-grey-brown color, exposed and shaggy where they emerge from the soil. From this base a dense thicket of slender, upright-to-arching twigs radiates upward and outward, dividing repeatedly into fine terminal shoots that give the whole canopy a soft, feathery, almost cloud-like outline with subtly ragged edges. The dominant surface material is the foliage: fine, narrow, slightly three-lobed or thread-like leaves packed tightly along the stems, coated in a pale silvery sage-green to whitish-grey tone produced by a fuzzy, downy pubescence that scatters light and mutes the green almost to dusty mint. The texture reads as velvety and matte, fine-grained and soft, with no glossy highlights. Closer to the interior and base, the woody stems show a rougher, stringy bark with peeling fibrous strands in muted browns and greys, contrasting the cool foliage with a warmer, drier woody note. The overall impression is of a drought-hardy aromatic bush. This kind of plant belongs to arid and semi-arid open country -- high desert basins, sagebrush steppe, dry rocky slopes and plains -- where pale silvery foliage and a wind-sculpted mounded habit are characteristic of cold-desert scrubland vegetation.
