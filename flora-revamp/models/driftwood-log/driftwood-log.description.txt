The main body reads as a smooth, sun- and water-worn log: a slightly curved, tubular mass that narrows to a blunt, rounded point at the left and broadens toward the right, where the wood erupts into a knotted clump of dozens of twisting, interlacing root fingers. These roots are gnarled and irregular, forking and curling over and under one another into a hollow, basket-like snarl with shadowed gaps between strands; some taper to broken, stubby tips while others arc back into the bundle. This kind of object belongs to riverbanks, lake shores, beaches, and arid washes where currents and weather strip, smooth, and bleach fallen wood; it also reads naturally as decorative driftwood for terrariums, aquariums, or rustic interior display.
