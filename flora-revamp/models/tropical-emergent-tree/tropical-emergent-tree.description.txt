A towering tropical emergent tree with the unmistakable silhouette of a kapok or ceiba: an exceptionally tall, slender, near-perfectly straight columnar trunk that rises clean and branchless for the great majority of its height before erupting into a broad, flattened, umbrella-like crown that spreads far wider than it is deep. The base flares dramatically into prominent plank buttresses — thin, fin-like roots that splay outward where trunk meets ground, giving the bottom a fluted, skirted profile that tapers rapidly into the smooth shaft above. The trunk itself is remarkably cylindrical with only gentle taper, its bark smooth to lightly mottled, pale grey to greenish-grey and silvery, marked with faint vertical streaking, subtle lenticel speckles, and occasional darker patches; young ceibas often bear conical thorns, hinted here as faint surface texture. High overhead the trunk divides into a few stout primary limbs that branch in horizontal, layered tiers, fanning into a wide, slightly domed canopy. The foliage forms dense, rounded, cloud-like clumps of mid- to deep-green palmate leaves, lighter and more yellow-green where sunlit at the crown's top and edges, deepening to shadowed green within. Two surface materials dominate: the smooth, cool grey-green woody bark cladding trunk and buttresses, and the soft, fine-textured green leaf mass billowing above. The overall impression is of immense height and lightness — a slim pale pillar crowned by a floating green parasol. This kind of giant belongs to lowland tropical rainforests and savanna margins, standing as an emergent that overtops the surrounding canopy in Central and South America, the Caribbean, and West Africa.
