A tree fern, immediately recognizable as a single stout columnar trunk crowned by a radiating rosette of large, arching, finely-divided fronds, giving the whole plant the silhouette of a feathery green fountain or shuttlecock spreading from a central point. The trunk is short, thick, and roughly cylindrical with only slight taper, standing as a solid pillar that occupies the lower third of the plant; its surface is a coarse, fibrous, shaggy material of densely matted root fibers and old frond bases, rich red-brown to dark chocolate in color, with a rough, hairy, almost coir-like or burlap texture rather than smooth bark. From the single growing crown at the trunk's top, a dozen or more fronds spring outward and upward in every direction, the central ones nearly vertical and the outer ones arching gracefully down and out, each frond a long bipinnate blade tapering to a slender tip. The foliage material is a bright, fresh yellow-green to mid-green, lightest and most lime-toned at the frond tips and youngest growth, slightly deeper green toward the interior shadowed clumps; the leaflets are narrow, lance-shaped, and serrated, lending a soft, lacy, feathered texture that catches light translucently at the margins. The contrast is the plant's defining character: a single rough dark trunk against an airy luminous green canopy many times its width. Tree ferns belong to cool, damp, shaded understory settings — temperate and tropical rainforests, fern gullies, stream banks, and humid sheltered gardens.
