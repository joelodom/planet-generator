A low, dome-shaped cushion-forming subshrub presenting as a single rounded mound that swells from a flat base into a soft, slightly asymmetrical hemisphere, broader than it is tall with a gently rolling crown rather than a sharp peak. There is no visible single trunk; instead the structure is a dense thicket of many fine, wiry, prostrate stems that radiate and creep outward from the center, branching repeatedly into a tight matted lattice so the interior reads as solid mass rather than open scaffolding. Clothing this framework is an extremely dense carpet of tiny, narrow, ovate leaves packed in small rounded clumps, giving the whole surface a fine pebbled, mossy, almost felted texture with countless small highlights and shadow pockets across the dome. The dominant surface material is this fresh mid- to yellow-green foliage, vivid and slightly glossy at the sunlit crown and deepening to a cooler, shadowed green toward the base and underside. Scattered evenly across and especially toward the upper third are many small flowers and buds in soft blush pink, pale rose, and near-white, clustered in little tufts that speckle the green like confetti and concentrate near the lit top. The overall palette is a warm green ground stippled with pink-white blossom and faint reddish stem tips. This is a mat-forming groundcover plant of sunny, well-drained places — rock gardens, alpine slopes, dry stone walls, gravel paths, and Mediterranean or temperate herb gardens — the kind of aromatic cushion that spreads between pavers and over banks in full sun.
