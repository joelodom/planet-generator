A tufted, grass-like sedge rising from a dense basal clump anchored in a small rounded plug of dark peaty soil and tangled pale roots. The overall silhouette is a fountain or explosion of slender vertical lines: a low, bushy skirt of fine arching leaf-blades at the base from which dozens of thin, wiry, near-vertical flowering stems shoot upward and gently splay outward, each terminating in a soft, rounded tuft. The structural breakdown is simple and repetitive — many independent culms emerging from one root mass, the lower third crowded with narrow grassy foliage and the upper two-thirds bare stalk carrying a single fluffy seed head apiece. The distinct surface materials are three: at the bottom, a crumbly clod of dark brown to near-black soil shot through with stringy beige roots; through the middle, the green plant matter — fine, blade-like leaves and stems in a fresh-to-yellowish mid-green, smooth and slightly glossy, growing paler and more straw-toned toward the tips; and crowning each stem, the signature cottony bristle heads, masses of fine white-to-cream filaments clustered into loose oval pom-poms with a soft, downy, slightly translucent texture like wisps of cotton wool or seed down. Color reads as a cool green base graduated into warm white highlights, with the muddy dark anchor grounding the airy top. This kind of plant belongs to cold, wet, open habitats — peat bogs, fens, marshy moorland, and arctic or alpine tundra — where it forms conspicuous white drifts across boggy ground in late spring and summer.
