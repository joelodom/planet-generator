A tall, single-trunk coniferous evergreen with a strongly conical, almost spire-like silhouette that tapers from a broad skirted base to a fine pointed leader at the crown, where a thin bare tip projects upward like a spindle. The form is classic excurrent: one dominant central trunk runs unbroken from ground to apex, with whorls of lateral branches radiating outward and gently sweeping downward, longest near the bottom and progressively shorter toward the top, producing a smooth pyramidal cone with a softly ragged, feathered outline. The dense branching holds short, stiff needles arranged in radial bottlebrush sprays, giving the canopy a fine-grained, plush, slightly frosted texture rather than a smooth surface. The dominant surface material is the foliage: a cool blue-green to silvery sage color, dusty and matte as if lightly powdered, packed into overlapping tiered clumps that catch light on their upper faces and fall into cooler shadow underneath. At the very bottom a short section of bare trunk is exposed, showing thin grey-brown to reddish bark with a dry, fibrous, lightly scaly texture; this woody column is slender relative to the great mass of needled branches it supports. The overall impression is crisp, orderly, and architectural, the kind of ornamental or wild conifer found in cool temperate and montane biomes, mountain slopes, northern forests, and cultivated landscapes, and instantly reads as a Christmas-tree archetype. Coloration stays within a restrained cool palette of blues, greys, and muted greens with no warm tones except the modest trunk.
