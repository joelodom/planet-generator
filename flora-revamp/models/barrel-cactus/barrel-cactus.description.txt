A solitary, nearly spherical barrel cactus presenting as a plump, self-contained globe that sits slightly wider than it is tall, giving it a squat, ball-like silhouette that bulges gently at the equator and rounds off smoothly at the crown. The body is built from a series of pronounced vertical ribs that run like meridians from the base up over the top, each rib a rounded, fleshy ridge separated from its neighbors by deep shadowed grooves; this accordion-like ribbing is the dominant structural motif and gives the surface a regular, segmented, melon-like organization. Along the apex of every rib sit evenly spaced areoles, and from each areole radiates a starburst cluster of stiff, straw-colored spines that splay outward in all directions, longer and straighter than they appear, overlapping between ribs to wrap the whole plant in a sparse golden cage. The crown is densely capped with a woolly tuft and a concentration of the youngest, brightest spines, forming a fuzzy yellow-gold pom at the very top where new growth emerges. The flesh material is a rich, waxy, saturated green—deeper and bluish in the grooves, brighter and slightly glossy on the rib crests—while the spine material is a warm amber-to-pale-gold, dry and bristly, catching light at the tips. The contrast between the smooth turgid green body and the prickly metallic-gold spination defines its character. This kind of plant belongs to arid desert and semidesert biomes, rocky sunbaked slopes and dry scrub, and is equally at home as a potted ornamental specimen in succulent gardens and sunny interiors.
