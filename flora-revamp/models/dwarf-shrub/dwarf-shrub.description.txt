A low, sprawling, mound-forming woody shrub far wider than it is tall, presenting as a loose hemispherical cushion of fine twiggy growth that hugs the ground and spreads outward in radiating fans. The structure begins from a short, barely visible woody base near the center from which numerous thin, wiry primary stems emerge and arch outward and slightly upward, repeatedly forking into ever-finer secondary and tertiary twigs that taper to delicate whip-like tips. The overall silhouette is broad, irregular, and slightly asymmetric — denser and taller through the middle, thinning toward the sprawling outer edges where bare twig ends reach laterally. Two distinct surface materials dominate. First, the woody stems and twigs: slender, smooth-to-finely-roughened bark in muted greyish-brown and pale tan tones, somewhat bleached and dry-looking, growing paler and more silvery toward the youngest growth. Second, the foliage: small, tight, ovate-to-rounded leaf clusters and buds scattered in loose rosettes along the upper twigs, soft sage-green to grey-green, slightly downy or matte rather than glossy, clumped in little tufts rather than forming a continuous canopy so that much of the twig framework remains visible through the sparse leaf cover. The leaves concentrate on the upper and outer twig surfaces, leaving the interior tangle of bare stems exposed. The texture character is dry, airy, brittle, and open — a drought-adapted look. This kind of low cushion shrub belongs to arid and semi-arid contexts: desert flats, sagebrush steppe, rocky scrubland, coastal dunes, and other exposed, sun-baked, low-water environments where vegetation grows sparse and ground-hugging.
