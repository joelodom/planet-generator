A dense clumping tussock of ornamental grass that springs from a single tight basal crown and fans upward and outward into a soft, vase-to-fountain silhouette. The overall form reads as an explosion of fine, hair-thin blades: narrow, linear leaves that rise nearly vertically near the center and progressively arch and splay toward the edges, the longest outermost blades curving gracefully and tapering to wispy, almost translucent points that drift and nod. There is no woody structure at all -- the entire plant is herbaceous, built only of a congested mound of overlapping leaf bases at the bottom that gathers into a slightly bushy, fibrous tuft, from which the slender culms and blades separate and diverge like jets of water. The blades themselves are filamentous and grass-like, with smooth, slightly glossy surfaces, each one a thin ribbon barely wider than a thread, bending under its own length. Coloration runs cool blue-green to grey-green and pale sage through the main body of the foliage, warming toward muted straw, tan, and golden-yellow at the dry, sun-bleached tips and along some of the longest arching blades, giving a feathery, faintly weathered appearance. The texture is fine, soft, and feathery in mass yet wiry up close, the kind of plant that catches light and moves in wind. The base is denser and a touch darker where blades crowd together. This sort of bunchgrass belongs in open sunny settings -- prairie and steppe, dune edges, dry meadows, gravelly borders, and ornamental garden beds where it is planted as a textural accent.
