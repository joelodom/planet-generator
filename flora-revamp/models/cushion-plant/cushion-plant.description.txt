A low, dome-shaped flowering cushion plant rendered as a single rounded mound, like a tight pillow or half-sphere of vegetation pressed close to the ground. The overall silhouette is a broad, gently flattened dome — wider than it is tall, swelling smoothly from a slightly tucked-under base up to a softly rounded crown with no central stem or dominant axis; the mass is the form. Structurally it reads as countless tiny stems and needle-fine leaves woven into a dense mat, so close-packed that no soil or interior structure shows through; the green understory functions as a continuous textured cushion rather than discrete branches. Erupting all over this mound, almost completely smothering it, are hundreds of small five-petalled flowers, each a simple flat star or rounded saucer of bloom held just above the foliage on short stalks. Two distinct surface materials are present: the foliage layer is a deep-to-mid green of fine, almost mossy texture, slightly darker and shadowed in the recesses between flower clusters; and the floral layer is a vivid magenta-to-rose pink, the petals carrying a soft satiny sheen with subtly paler centers and warmer saturated edges, clustered most densely across the top and upper flanks where light catches them and thinning slightly toward the shaded lower rim. The texture overall is fine-grained and stippled, a confetti-like scatter of pink over a velvety green ground. This kind of plant belongs to alpine, rockery, and cottage-garden settings — a mat-forming perennial like moss phlox, creeping thrift, or dianthus that spills over stone walls, edges paths, and carpets sunny well-drained slopes in spring bloom.
