A tall, slender tropical palm with the unmistakable silhouette of a single unbranched trunk crowned by a radiating burst of feathery fronds. The overall form reads as a thin vertical stem topped by a near-hemispherical to fountain-like spray of foliage that arches outward and droops gracefully at the tips, giving the canopy a soft, shaggy, wind-swept character. The trunk is the dominant structural element: a smooth-to-roughly-ringed cylindrical column that tapers gently as it rises, leaning with a subtle natural curve rather than standing perfectly plumb, and widening slightly at the swollen base where it meets the ground. Its surface is a pale grey-tan to weathered brown bark marked by faint horizontal scars and ring-like leaf-base nodes left where old fronds have fallen, lending a banded, fibrous texture. At the crown, a tight cluster of leaf bases bundles together into a thickened green-brown 'shuttlecock' hub, from which perhaps a dozen-plus long pinnate fronds spring in all directions. Each frond is a central rachis lined with hundreds of narrow, blade-like leaflets, creating a fine, comb-like or feathered texture; the foliage color ranges from fresh yellow-green at the sunlit outer tips to deeper saturated mid-green toward the shaded interior, with the lower, older fronds sagging downward while upper ones reach skyward. The texture contrast is strong: the hard, matte, woody trunk against the soft, glossy, finely-divided leaf mass. This kind of tree belongs to coastal tropical and subtropical biomes — sandy beaches, island shores, resort plantings, and lowland equatorial landscapes.
