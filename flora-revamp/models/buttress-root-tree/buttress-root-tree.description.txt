A tall, slender tropical canopy tree with a markedly columnar, emergent silhouette: a single dominant trunk rises almost ramrod-straight through most of the frame before splitting into a relatively compact, high-set crown, giving the whole form a top-heavy lollipop-on-a-pole profile typical of emergent rainforest giants reaching for light. The most distinctive structural feature is the base, where the trunk flares dramatically into broad, thin, wing-like buttress roots that splay outward and meet the ground in fluted, plank-like fins — a wide, anchoring skirt that contrasts sharply with the narrow shaft above. Above the buttresses the trunk shows only gentle taper, staying clean and branch-free for a long stretch (a self-pruned bole), then giving rise to a few ascending primary limbs that fork into finer, somewhat zig-zagging secondary and tertiary branches supporting the foliage. The crown is irregular and airy rather than perfectly rounded, built from loose, billowing clumps of leaves with visible gaps of sky between them. Surface materials: the bark is smooth to lightly mottled, pale grey shading to greenish-tan and warmer brown in the buttressed lower trunk, with subtle vertical streaking and faint lichen blotching; the buttress fins read as the same bark stretched over thin blades. The foliage is medium-to-dark green, finely textured, with cooler bluish-green shadows and brighter sunlit highlights at the crown's upper edges. This kind of tree belongs to lowland tropical and subtropical rainforests, standing as an emergent above the surrounding canopy in humid, biodiverse forest.
